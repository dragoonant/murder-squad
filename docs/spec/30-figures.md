# 30 — Figures, attachments and terrain

Owner decisions (2026-10-17): fresh MGSD figures for every operative; **modular**: a body per operative archetype plus weapon and equipment attachments on sockets, showing the exact rules-legal loadout chosen in the team builder. Concepts from Gemini only (placeholders until a Gemini session exists). Pipeline details: `docs/knowledge-base/local-ai-studios.md`.

## 1. Style
MGSD (Bandai Master Grade SD): about 2–2.5 heads tall, head about 40% of total height and as wide as the shoulders, tiny torso, short stubby legs with big chunky feet, short arms with oversized hands, Master Grade hard-surface panel detail, plain round black base, white background, no lettering. Two dominant hues per team (for the hue-band army painter): Angels of Death deep blue / gold; Kommandos green skin and black-red scrap. Check proportions in the image, not the prompt; reject extra limbs/weapons, fused parts, text, cropped bases.

## 2. Body contract (`public/assets/models/bodies/<archetype>.glb`)
- Origin at base centre, y up, 1 unit = 1 inch, front +Z, pre-scaled to the datacard height (`heightIn` in the manifest), black base disc added upstream by `stage_finalize.py`.
- **Bare-handed**: the Gemini concept asks for empty hands in the hold pose (right hand a loose fist as if gripping, left hand open or supporting). Hunyuan meshes are fused, so sockets are measured after generation (`tools/models/sockets.mjs` reads a hand-picked point from a side/front render or from the manifest) and stored in the manifest, not in the GLB.
- Manifest `public/assets/models/manifest.json`: `{ bodies: { <archetype>: { file, heightIn, baseMm, sockets: { handR, handL, back, beltR, beltL, shoulderL, shoulderR, head }, poses: { long?, heavy?, melee2h?, shield? } } }, attachments: { <weaponOrEquipId>: { file, holdClass: pistol|rifle|long|heavy|melee1h|melee2h|shield|pack|grenade|tool, grip: Vec3, support?: Vec3, scale } } }`.
- Pose variants (`<archetype>@long.glb`, `@heavy.glb`, `@melee2h.glb`): the base concept edited in Gemini into the two-handed stance, regenerated through Hunyuan, both grip and support sockets measured (Fell-in M5 method). An operative whose chosen loadout needs a class the body lacks falls back to the default pose with the single grip socket.

## 3. Attachments (`public/assets/models/attachments/<id>.glb`)
One GLB per weapon or visible equipment: bolt rifle, auto bolt rifle, stalker bolt rifle, heavy bolt pistol, plasma pistol, hand flamer, chainsword, power fist, power weapon, thunder hammer; slugga, choppa, big choppa, power klaw, burna, rokkit launcha, breacha ram, snipa, dakka shoota, grot-related bits; grenades (belt), comms pack (back), medi-pack… Concept: side view, horizontal, barrel pointing right, no hands, white background. Scale: weapon length in inches relative to the body height (data in manifest). Attachment GLBs are also fused Hunyuan meshes, flat-tinted when paint fails.

## 4. Rendering (`src/client/figures/`)
`Operative.tsx` picks the body (archetype + pose by the loadout's heaviest hold class), mounts attachments at sockets with per-class offset/rotation (`HOLDS` table; attachments are authored with the origin at the bottom of the grip and the fists are about 0.2" across, so every hand-held class sinks the grip 0.08" below the socket so the handle runs through the fist; rifles and long guns are carried muzzle-down, pistols level, melee blades hang down-forward from the left fist), applies the team paint shader (hue-band remap of the two source hues), and plays rigid whole-body cues (idle breathe, walk bob, shoot recoil, melee lunge, hit flinch, death topple/fade). Procedural stand-in (`Procedural.tsx`: capsule body, box weapon by hold class, coloured by team) while a GLB is missing or loading. `?gallery` shows every body × pose × attachment for the QC pass.

## 5. Terrain
Volkus-style set: stronghold, large ruin (two floors with vantage), small ruin, heavy rubble, light rubble, barricades, containers, pipes; each piece a GLB fitted to its rules footprint (`terrainFit.ts`) with traits per part from the killzone layout data. Concepts via Gemini ("fully painted tabletop wargame terrain piece, grimdark ruined gothic-industrial, white background"), Hunyuan, decimated to about 6k triangles; flats (rubble patches, floor plates) procedural. Vantage floors are explicit placeable surfaces in data so the engine and the renderer agree on heights.

## 7. Skeletal animation (M8)

Owner decision (2026-10-17): aim for true skeletal rigging with UniRig, part-split articulation as the fallback; run the GPU work on Alteisen.

### 7.1 Pilot result: UniRig does not rig MGSD bodies, the procedural rig ships
UniRig (VAST-AI, SIGGRAPH'25) was installed on Alteisen in its own venv (`E:\AI\UniRig`, Python 3.11, torch 2.4.0+cu124, flash-attn 2.7.4 Windows wheel, spconv-cu124, PyG wheels, bpy 4.2, triton-windows 3.8; about 7 minutes, see the hand-over note for the three Windows gotchas: `triton` is needed by transformers' flash helper, the venv launcher fails over ssh so the base interpreter is called with `PYTHONPATH`, and `2>&1 | findstr` on a remote cmd line swallows all later output). The skeleton checkpoint (`VAST-AI/UniRig` on Hugging Face) auto-downloads; a skeleton prediction takes about 25 s per body on the RTX 3080 and the skin prediction about 35 s.

On `aod-intercessor-warrior` and `kom-boy` the autoregressive skeleton model returned **three joints on a straight line through the body's axis** (root, mid, top) in every setting tried: the default `articulationxl` class, the trained `vroid` class (anime proportions), class-free, beam search with repetition penalty 3.0 / 1.0, greedy decoding, with and without the base disc in the input, seed 12345 (10 skeleton runs per body). The one exception, `kom-boy` with the figure-only input, `vroid` class and repetition penalty 1.0, gave 13 joints: a spine, four sideways arm-like chains at mid-height and one chain into the backpack, and no legs, so it cannot carry a walk either. The three-bone output breaks UniRig's own FBX export (`index 3 is out of bounds` with `group_per_vertex=4`), so the skin stage writes only `predict_skin.npz` and the merge step has nothing to load. The 2-head-tall fused Hunyuan meshes with limbs pressed against the torso are read as a single rigid object; the model was trained on Articulation-XL and VRoid rigs with real limb separation. **Conclusion: UniRig is not usable for this figure style without a different mesh (separated limbs) or fine-tuning; no further time spent on it.**

### 7.2 What shipped: a procedural humanoid rig with soft planar cuts
`tools/models/split-body.py <archetype> [--layout auto|humanoid|biped] [--band 0.03]` builds a VRoid-named skeleton from the manifest sockets and measured heights. The **humanoid** layout has 17 joints (hips, spine, chest, neck, head; upper arm / lower arm / hand per side; upper leg / lower leg / foot per side): hips just under the belt sockets, knees at 45% of the leg, neck on the shoulder line, shoulders and hands on the `shoulderL/R` and `handL/R` sockets, elbows between. Skin weights are assigned by region with planar cuts softened over a 3%-of-height blend band: the torso-to-arm cut is the density valley of |x| across the chest band, left/right at x = 0, upper/lower limbs at the elbow and knee heights, head above the neck line. The valley is searched between 55% and 75% of the hand socket's |x| (the one socket that always sits on the arm; shoulder sockets sit on pauldrons, packs or ears, the comms boy's is at 0.16" against a hand at 0.38") and falls back to 63% of it when the chest band has no interior minimum, which is where both pilot bodies' accepted cuts landed (0.275" / 0.429" and 0.327" / 0.512"). The roll-out needed that window: the unconstrained valley search put the cut at 0.06" to 0.17" on eight bodies whose weapon crosses the chest (the arms then swallowed the torso) and at 0.37" on the assault warrior (51 vertices left for the right upper arm). The **biped** layout (11 joints, no arms; picked automatically when a body has no hand sockets) is for the bomb squig: two legs under a body with a tail and a jaw, the legs being the vertices below the hip line inside the z window the feet occupy on the base, tail and jaw riding the hips. This is the part-split route expressed as skin weights: the same articulation as cutting the mesh into parts, without the parts tearing apart at the cuts. The base disc stays a separate, unskinned mesh.

`tools/models/rig-clips.py <in.glb> <out.glb>` adds the motion clips and the hold poses (section 7.5) to any skinned body GLB; the six motion clips: `idle` (2.4 s loop: breathing, head sway), `walk` (0.6 s loop, two steps: 26 degree thigh swing, knee bend on the forward swing, arm counter-swing, hip bob and roll), `shoot` (0.24 s gun-arm kick and lean), `melee` (0.38 s wind-up and left-arm strike with a torso twist), `hit` (0.30 s backward flinch with a damped wobble), `death` (0.60 s topple backwards about the heels with folding knees). Clip lengths equal the client's cue lengths (`CUE_MS`, the fall part of `DEATH_MS`). Rotations are authored about world axes at each joint's rest pivot and converted to the joint's local frame, so the tool is independent of bone conventions; joints are found by VRoid names first and by geometry (trunk / legs / arms from the branch directions) otherwise. Output: `public/assets/models/bodies/rigged/<slug>.glb` (1.7 to 1.8 MB each; the unrigged bodies stay in place for the rigid fallback), manifest `bodies.<slug>.rigged` and `rig` (the `rig.tool` string records the layout and the arm cut with how it was found).

`tools/models/skin-glb.py` is the UniRig path (npz skeleton + sampled weights into the original GLB, KD-tree weight transfer with the 48-way axis search UniRig's merge uses); it refuses a skeleton with fewer than four joints and is kept for a future model that produces limbs. `tools/models/glbio.py` is the shared GLB reader/writer.

### 7.3 Client
- `src/client/figures/skinned.tsx`: `SkinnedBody` plays the rigged GLB through an `AnimationMixer` driven by the same cue store as the rigid cues (`cueCore.ts`): `idle`/`walk` loop as the base layer and cross-fade on the walking flag (move tween or `setWalking`), `shoot`/`melee`/`hit` run once at full weight with a 20% ramp while `cueState` reports them (time-scaled to `CUE_MS`), `death` plays once and clamps, started at its true age when the corpse layer remounts the body. Attachments are portalled into a group under the bone nearest each manifest socket in the rest pose (`bindSockets`), axes aligned with the display frame, so a weapon follows the hand.
- `cuePose(id, now, walking, phase, skinned)`: the reduced pose for a skinned body keeps only the aim turn while alive and, for a death, the travel, sink and fade (the fall is the clip's job; blast keeps its tumble, burn its collapse). `GlbBody` switches to the skinned body once `manifest.rigged` is loaded (`isSkinned`); until then, and for every body without a rig, the rigid cues run unchanged.
- `?gallery`: "Animate" runs a scripted loop (walk bursts, one-shots, a death and reset every 20 ticks, `galleryAnim.ts`), "Bones" draws the skeleton, labels read "rigged GLB"; `?gallery&only=<archetype>&zoom=0.22&view=three-quarter` frames one figure for close-ups (`tools/out/rig-closeup.mjs`). `window.__ms.riggedLoaded()` for e2e.

### 7.5 Hold poses (weapons in the hands)
The bodies are modelled bare-handed with the fists at the sides, so an unrigged body can only carry its weapon at the hip (HOLDS sinks the grip into the fist and tilts rifles muzzle-down). A rigged body poses its arms per weapon class: `rig-clips.py` writes static clips `hold-<class>` (pistol, rifle, long, heavy on the right arm; melee1h, melee2h, tool on the left), `support-<class>` (the free left hand on a two-handed gun; the right hand on a two-handed melee weapon) and `rest`. The client (`skinned.tsx`) plays the hold and support clips for the carried weapons as the base layer and converts the motion clips (idle, walk, shoot, melee, hit, death) to additive against `rest` so they stack on the pose. Angles are authored about world axes at the rest pivots: a negative X on a hanging arm swings it forward, the hand joint gets the opposite X turn so the grip stays upright and the barrel level; the across-the-chest turn is a shoulder yaw (a hand yaw would act about the forearm's rest axis, which after the elbow bend is a roll). The arms are too short for a hand on the forestock of a 0.9" gun held straight out, so rifles and heavy weapons are carried at port arms (grip at the belly, barrel across the chest, support hand in front of the body), pistols are aimed forward at shoulder height, blades are raised in front. The measured hand sockets (`measure-sockets.py`) sit on the fist's outer face, about 0.1" outboard of its centre; `split-body.py --write-sockets` finds the fist centre on the mesh (mean-shift into the hand blob on the arm side of the cut, lowest 0.16 H slab), puts the hand joint there and writes it back as the manifest's `handL` / `handR` (the measured values are kept under `measuredHandSockets` and still anchor the arm cut), so the weapon mount is in the middle of the closed hand on rigged bodies. Tune with `node tools/out/weapon-qc.mjs <archetype>` (side / front / left captures with sockets) and `node tools/out/rig-measure.mjs <archetype>` (posed hand bones and weapon orientation).

### 7.4 Quality notes and next steps
- Close-ups (e2e-out/rig-closeup-*.png): strides, arm swings, head turns and the backward topple read well at tabletop scale; the soft cuts stretch a few millimetres of armour at the shoulder and hip seams on extreme poses, invisible at battle camera distance. Pauldrons move with the upper arm (they fall outside the arm cut) which is right for a Space Marine.
- Weapons stay in hand because the hand joints sit on the measured sockets; the melee strike moves the left-hand weapon through the cut, as intended.
- Rolled out (2026-10-17, M8 roll-out): all 20 bodies in the manifest are rigged, 19 with the humanoid layout and the bomb squig with the biped layout. The grot takes the humanoid layout as it is: its arms hang outboard of its legs down to knee height, so the planar arm cut (0.19", the 63% fallback) separates them the whole way. The bomb squig turned out to be two-legged (feet at x = ±0.25" on the base, a tail behind and the jaw in front), so no quadruped layout was needed; its walk is a two-step shuffle under the bobbing body, the arm channels of the clips are simply absent (`rig-clips.py` tolerates empty arm lists), and shoot never fires on it anyway. Nothing stayed rigid.
- Per body: `python tools/models/split-body.py <archetype> && python tools/models/rig-clips.py public/assets/models/bodies/rigged/<archetype>.glb public/assets/models/bodies/rigged/<archetype>.glb`, then `rigged` and `rig` in the manifest (`rig.tool` carries the cut and how it was found, `valley` or `default (no valley)`), and a look at `?gallery&only=<archetype>&zoom=0.22&view=three-quarter` with Animate and Bones on (`tools/out/rig-closeup.mjs`, gitignored, captures eight frames per body). The cut was read off the split report's `dominant_vertices_per_joint` first: a healthy humanoid has roughly 1-2.5k vertices on each upper leg, 0.4-3k on each upper arm and 3-7k on the spine and chest; an arm over 4k with a hip under 500 means the cut ran inside the torso. Pose variants (`@long`, `@heavy`) would get the same treatment per file.
- The cut rule leaves a known soft spot: on the pose variants whose weapon crosses the chest (eliminator, intercessor gunner, heavy gunner, comms, snipa, slasha, captain) the valley search finds no interior minimum and the 63% fallback is used; those arms carry a sliver of chest plate, invisible at battle camera distance. A wider blend band (`--band 0.04`) is the first thing to try if a seam shows on one of them.
- Still rigid: idle breathing shows only on frames the board draws anyway (same rule as before); the shoot clip does not yet differ by hold class (a heavy weapon would want a two-handed brace).
